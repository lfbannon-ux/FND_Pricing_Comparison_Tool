### 1.3 The ownership stack as at the AIF date

Stated directly at [p.5](https://web.quartr.com/companies/21558?companyId=21558&documentId=3724816&documentType=report&eventId=554427&navigation=external&utm_medium=referral&utm_source=mcp&rp=5):

- **Brookfield owns** (i) **all** of the outstanding **Class B Shares**, representing **60%** of the aggregate number of
  outstanding Shares and voting interests in the Company; and (ii) **60% of the outstanding OpCo Interests**.
- **The public owns all** of the outstanding **Class A Shares**, representing **40%** of the aggregate number of
  outstanding Shares and voting interests in the Company.
- **The Company owns 40% of the outstanding OpCo Interests.**

**Consequence, in the AIF's own words:** "Brookfield **controls** the Company and the OpCos and therefore the Business,
which is carried on by Rockpoint Gas Storage. The Company holds the remaining **40% interest in the Business** through
its direct ownership of 40% of the outstanding OpCo Interests and its resulting **indirect ownership of 40% of the
outstanding interests of the other Rockpoint Gas Storage entities**" [p.5](https://web.quartr.com/companies/21558?companyId=21558&documentId=3724816&documentType=report&eventId=554427&navigation=external&utm_medium=referral&utm_source=mcp&rp=5).

Confirmed again at [p.57](https://web.quartr.com/companies/21558?companyId=21558&documentId=3724816&documentType=report&eventId=554427&navigation=external&utm_medium=referral&utm_source=mcp&rp=57) (Promoters): as of the AIF date **Brookfield Infrastructure beneficially owns 79,800,000
Class B Shares, being 100% of the Class B Shares and 60% of the total outstanding Shares and voting interests**.

**Effective economic interest — the chain that matters for the model:**

```
Public shareholder
   └─ owns Class A Shares  ── 53,200,000 of 133,000,000 total Shares = 40.0% of the Company's votes
        └─ Rockpoint Gas Storage Inc. (Alberta)
             └─ owns 40% of the OpCo Interests  (40% of Swan OpCo Units + 40% of BIF OpCo Shares)
                  └─ 40% indirect interest in every Rockpoint Gas Storage entity
                       └─ 40% economic interest in the Business (100% basis)

Brookfield
   └─ owns 100% of Class B Shares = 60% of the Company's votes   (Class B carry NO dividend/economic entitlement)
   └─ owns 60% of the OpCo Interests DIRECTLY, alongside the Company — NOT through the Company
        └─ 60% economic interest in the Business (100% basis)
```

The critical structural point: **Brookfield's 60% of the Business does not sit under Rockpoint Gas Storage Inc.** It sits
*beside* it, held directly at the OpCo level. Rockpoint Gas Storage Inc.'s only asset is the 40% OpCo stake; its Class B
Shares give Brookfield **votes without economics** in the Company (Class B holders "are not entitled to receive any
dividends or other distributions", other than a **C$0.000001 per share** liquidation preference) [p.49](https://web.quartr.com/companies/21558?companyId=21558&documentId=3724816&documentType=report&eventId=554427&navigation=external&utm_medium=referral&utm_source=mcp&rp=49). This is why
the AIF describes the Company as "fully dependent upon the financial results and cash flows of Rockpoint Gas Storage and
distributions it receives from the OpCos" [p.44](https://web.quartr.com/companies/21558?companyId=21558&documentId=3724816&documentType=report&eventId=554427&navigation=external&utm_medium=referral&utm_source=mcp&rp=44).

of the Business are those of **Swan Equity Aggregator LP *and* BIF II CalGas (Delaware) LLC and their wholly-owned
subsidiaries** — the two legs have to be combined to see the whole Business.

Rockpoint Gas Storage operating entity under Swan OpCo, and a **borrower under both credit facilities**. RGSP LP is also

- The **BIF OpCo Board** = three managers; the **Swan GP Board** = three directors.

### 3.4 Commercial model

Revenue is categorised in two buckets, with **three** contracting strategies [p.17](https://web.quartr.com/companies/21558?companyId=21558&documentId=3724816&documentType=report&eventId=554427&navigation=external&utm_medium=referral&utm_source=mcp&rp=17):

- **Fee-for-Service Revenue** — from storage arrangements: **long-term ToP contracts** *and* **recurring STS contracts**.
- **Optimization Revenue** — from using a portion of available capacity for activities with **proprietary owned gas**.

**Capacity allocation waterfall** (this is the key operating logic) [p.17](https://web.quartr.com/companies/21558?companyId=21558&documentId=3724816&documentType=report&eventId=554427&navigation=external&utm_medium=referral&utm_source=mcp&rp=17):
> Storage capacity is **initially reserved to service ToP customer obligations** based on ToP customers' expected
> seasonal injection/withdrawal patterns. **STS contracts fill capacity not utilised under ToP contracting.** **Any
> remaining capacity is used for optimization** (subject to injection/withdrawal rate availability during the year).

**Take-or-Pay (ToP)** [p.18](https://web.quartr.com/companies/21558?companyId=21558&documentId=3724816&documentType=report&eventId=554427&navigation=external&utm_medium=referral&utm_source=mcp&rp=18):
- Fixed **monthly demand charge** for capacity, **regardless of utilisation**; right to inject/store/withdraw a
  predetermined amount.
- **Additional variable fees** payable on actual volumes injected or withdrawn when reserved capacity is used.
- Contracts define both total capacity and **daily withdrawal and injection rights**, which fluctuate with utilisation.
- Typical term **one to ten years** [p.17](https://web.quartr.com/companies/21558?companyId=21558&documentId=3724816&documentType=report&eventId=554427&navigation=external&utm_medium=referral&utm_source=mcp&rp=17).
- **Contracting cadence:** typically entered into in the **fall and winter** for service commencing the **following
  April** and beyond — letting customers use the full **summer injection season (April 1 – October 31)** before the
  **winter withdrawal season (November 1 – March 31)**.

**Short-Term Storage (STS)** [p.18](https://web.quartr.com/companies/21558?companyId=21558&documentId=3724816&documentType=report&eventId=554427&navigation=external&utm_medium=referral&utm_source=mcp&rp=18):
- Fixed fee to inject and withdraw specified quantities, **typically 50% payable on injection and 50% on withdrawal**.
- Unlike ToP, customers **must** inject and withdraw **predetermined quantities on specified dates**.
- Captures **seasonal summer/winter price differentials**, net of the customer's required return.
- Because volumes and timing are fixed, the Company can **opportunistically execute offsetting transactions** to capture
  incremental storage value as spot and forward spreads move before the scheduled withdrawal date.
- Typical term: **up to one storage season**, with a "**strong history of contract renewals**" [p.17](https://web.quartr.com/companies/21558?companyId=21558&documentId=3724816&documentType=report&eventId=554427&navigation=external&utm_medium=referral&utm_source=mcp&rp=17).

**Optimization** [p.18](https://web.quartr.com/companies/21558?companyId=21558&documentId=3724816&documentType=report&eventId=554427&navigation=external&utm_medium=referral&utm_source=mcp&rp=18):
- Purchasing, storing and selling natural gas **for the Company's own account using its corporate liquidity**.
- The **Risk Policy** "restricts taking open positions that create exposure to price or physical delivery risk, subject
  to certain limited exceptions". Market price risk is mitigated by **matching inventory purchases with corresponding
  physical and financial contracts, effectively locking in margins**. Activities are described as
  "**non-speculative in nature**".
- Three named strategies: (i) capturing the spread from **short-term cash-market weakness vs forward markets**;
  (ii) **selling inventory in cash markets during high price events while simultaneously repurchasing lower-priced gas
  in forward markets**; (iii) **intra-season injections and withdrawals**.
- Explicitly "a **small portion**" of storage capacity.

**Contracting mix table** [p.17](https://web.quartr.com/companies/21558?companyId=21558&documentId=3724816&documentType=report&eventId=554427&navigation=external&utm_medium=referral&utm_source=mcp&rp=17) — this is the single most model-relevant table in the AIF. Every column foots to 100%,

### 3.9 Seasonality and cycles

The AIF's own seasonality section is **one paragraph and defers to the MD&A**: "The Business is seasonal, reflecting the
impact of weather-driven patterns on natural gas demand and pricing. For additional information… please see the
'**Organizational Overview – Seasonality of the Business**' section of the **Annual MD&A**, which section is incorporated
by reference" [p.26](https://web.quartr.com/companies/21558?companyId=21558&documentId=3724816&documentType=report&eventId=554427&navigation=external&utm_medium=referral&utm_source=mcp&rp=26).

The substantive seasonality description sits in the **risk factors** [p.34](https://web.quartr.com/companies/21558?companyId=21558&documentId=3724816&documentType=report&eventId=554427&navigation=external&utm_medium=referral&utm_source=mcp&rp=34):
- **Revenue typically moderately higher in Q3 and Q4 (November through March)** — the peak of the winter withdrawal
  season, when most optimization inventory is sold. **STS revenue is weighted to the shorter withdrawal season versus
  the longer injection season.**
- **Revenue typically lower in Q1 and Q2 (April through October)** — prices generally lower; the Business shifts to the
  injection season and replenishes inventory.
- **Working capital pattern:** gas is purchased and inventories built during summer while financial sales are hedged
  forward. This "can result in **borrowing under the Revolving Credit Facility being higher as the amount of proprietary
  inventory increases**". Borrowings can also rise with **margin posting requirements** on future purchase and sale
  commitments, even with a locked-in margin and physical inventory in hand.
- **Greatest accounts receivable collections typically occur in Q4**, to the extent physical inventory is sold.
- With lower summer cash flow, "Rockpoint Gas Storage and/or the Company **may be required to borrow money during such
  periods** to fund operations and maintain their capital allocation strategy".
- **Inter-year deferral risk:** "If winter gas prices fall below forward prices for the following summer, Rockpoint Gas
  Storage **may defer the withdrawal of proprietary optimization inventory until the next financial year**… This may
  result in the **deferral of realized earnings and cash flow from one financial year to the next and cause volatility
  in the Company's financial results and cash flow**" [p.32](https://web.quartr.com/companies/21558?companyId=21558&documentId=3724816&documentType=report&eventId=554427&navigation=external&utm_medium=referral&utm_source=mcp&rp=32).
